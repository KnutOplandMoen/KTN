The big picture

Before you dive into the details — here is the map of the terrain.

When you open Netflix, your laptop sends small chunks of data — packets — that travel through perhaps ten to fifteen routers before reaching a Netflix server somewhere else in the world. The server sends video packets back, and they find their way through an entirely different set of routers. All of this happens in a few tens of milliseconds, governed by a set of rules called protocols. Chapter 1 is about understanding this machinery at a high level: what is the Internet made of, how does data move, and why is everything organized the way it is?

The analogy you need

Think of the Internet as a postal system. Your house and mine are end systems (hosts). The post offices are routers that sort and forward. The letters are packets — data divided into manageable chunks with an address label (header) at the front. And the shared rules for how to address, stamp, and deliver mail are protocols. You drop the letter in the mailbox (the socket) and trust that the system delivers — without knowing which route it takes.

A crucial insight is that the Internet uses packet switching, not circuit switching. This means that a dedicated connection is never set up between you and the server — your packets share the links with everyone else, and capacity is allocated dynamically. This is why the Internet can serve billions of users without reserving a line for every single conversation. The price is that delays can vary and packets can be lost — but the gain in efficiency is enormous.

Why does this matter?

Everything you learn in the rest of the course — HTTP, TCP, IP, Ethernet — is one layer in this stack. Once you understand the layering, you also understand why the course is structured the way it is: we start at the top (applications you know) and work our way down to the bits on the wire. Chapter 1 gives you the entire map; the remaining chapters zoom in on each layer.

Test yourself

Check whether you have understood the most important concepts from this chapter.

Question 1 · Easy
Which statement best describes the difference between packet switching and circuit switching?
Packet switching shares capacity dynamically among users, while circuit switching reserves capacity per connection.
Question 2 · Medium
In practice, which statement about what a network protocol actually defines is most correct?
A protocol defines message format, ordering, and the actions performed on send and receive.
Question 3 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of the role of end systems in the Internet is most accurate?
End systems run applications and act as data sources/destinations, while routers only forward packets.
Question 4 · Medium
In practice, which statement about the store-and-forward principle is most correct?
A router must receive the full packet before it can forward it on the next link.
Question 5 · Easy
Which statement best describes how transmission delay is computed?
Transmission delay is L/R, i.e. packet size in bits divided by link rate.
Question 6 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of how propagation delay is computed is most accurate?
Propagation delay is distance divided by signal speed in the medium.
Question 7 · Medium
In practice, which statement about why queueing delay is hard to predict is most correct?
Queueing delay varies with instantaneous traffic load and the number of packets already waiting.
Question 8 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of what traffic intensity La/R tells you is most accurate?
La/R measures link load; when it approaches 1, queues grow sharply.
Question 9 · Easy
Which statement best describes what a bottleneck link is?
The bottleneck link has the lowest capacity on the path and limits end-to-end throughput.
Question 10 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of statistical multiplexing is most accurate?
Statistical multiplexing exploits users being active at different times so capacity can be shared efficiently.
Question 11 · Medium
In practice, which statement about why packet drops happen in routers is most correct?
Packets are dropped when buffers are full and arrivals exceed the outgoing service rate.
Question 12 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of how traceroute reveals path hops is most accurate?
Traceroute increases TTL step by step so each intermediate router returns ICMP time exceeded.
Question 13 · Easy
Which statement best describes what ping actually measures?
Ping measures round-trip time (RTT) between ICMP echo request and echo reply.
Question 14 · Medium
In practice, which statement about why layering is used in networking is most correct?
Layering isolates complexity, makes design modular, and allows layers to evolve independently.
Question 15 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of encapsulation in the protocol stack is most accurate?
Each layer adds its own header around data from the layer above.
Question 16 · Medium
In practice, which statement about what an access network is is most correct?
The access network connects an end system to the ISP's first router.
Question 17 · Easy
Which statement best describes why DSL is often asymmetric?
DSL typically allocates more downstream than upstream capacity because user traffic is download-heavy.
Question 18 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of what it means that cable access is a shared medium is most accurate?
Multiple homes share the same channel, so user rate varies with neighbors' activity.
Question 19 · Medium
In practice, which statement about why fiber is used in the Internet core is most correct?
Fiber provides high capacity, low attenuation, and strong immunity to electromagnetic noise.
Question 20 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of how the ISP hierarchy is organized is most accurate?
Access ISPs connect to regional/tier-1 networks and exchange traffic via peering and transit.
Question 21 · Medium
In practice, which statement about the role of IXPs is most correct?
IXPs let networks exchange traffic directly, reducing cost and often delay.
Question 22 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of what a botnet does in a DDoS attack is most accurate?
A botnet coordinates many compromised machines to flood a target with traffic.
Question 23 · Easy
Which statement best describes what IP spoofing means?
IP spoofing is sending packets with a forged source address to hide or mislead sender identity.
Question 24 · Medium
In practice, which statement about why core network devices are kept simple is most correct?
Simple core data-plane operations improve scalability and forwarding speed.
Question 25 · Hard
In a larger network setting, which evaluation of what the socket interface gives applications is most accurate?
The socket interface lets applications send/receive network data without implementing routing themselves.
